# Communication: the ladder of abstraction

Most misunderstandings are two people standing on different rungs.

- **Climb down** to concrete: which report, how many seconds, compared with what.
- **Climb up** to meaning: what these cases share and why it matters.

Going down is not adding every detail. Choose the angle this listener can act on, then make that angle specific.

| Audience | Keep | Drop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive | Shared pattern, consequence, decision needed | Mechanisms, individual traits that distinguish nothing |
| Engineering | Observed behaviour, steps, conditions, numbers, constraints | Long persona description, business framing alone |
| Design | Situation, motivation, information priority, evidence | Implementation detail |
| Marketing and sales | Who, what job, their own words | Technical measurements |

Handing someone raw transcripts or a hundred documents moves the selection work to the person with the least context.

When someone does not understand, ask: which rung are they on, and how far can I climb toward them? On the receiving end, climb too: read the goal, flow and test cases first when buried in detail; draw the flow, list reproduction steps and attach screenshots when reporting a bug. Re-align on expectations only after you have done your half.

Prefer a small written artifact (a concept diagram, a one-page memo) over a verbal explanation when agreement needs to be remembered.

Before drafting any message, establish: who reads it, what they should decide or do, and what they already know.
